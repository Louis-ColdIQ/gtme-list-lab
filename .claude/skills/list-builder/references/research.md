# How to research (free, public sources only)

This lab uses pages anyone can open in a browser. No logins to paid data tools, no API keys, no scraping behind a login wall.

## Good sources

- The company's own website: homepage, about, pricing, team or leadership, careers
- Public "alternatives to" or comparison pages
- Public directories and official registries (for example Companies House in the UK, or a trade association member list)
- News articles and press releases
- A public LinkedIn company page, if it opens without signing in. If it asks the student to log in, skip the page and leave `linkedin_url` or employee count blank.

## How to find the first companies

Start from the ICP, not from a name you already know. Useful searches:

- `best [industry] tools for [country or size]`
- `[industry] companies in [country]`
- `alternatives to [one well known product in the category]`
- `[industry] "customers" OR "case study"` to find vendors, then check each vendor's site

For each candidate, open the company website and answer: do they sell this kind of product, in this kind of market, at this kind of size? If you cannot tell from public pages, skip them.

## Employee count and LinkedIn

Only fill `employee_count` when a public page states it, and note that page as the source. A careers page that lists 3 open roles is not a headcount. Do not guess.

Only fill a LinkedIn URL you actually opened or that the official website links to.

## People

Prefer a public team, about, or leadership page on the company website. Take 1 or 2 people whose titles match the ICP.

If no public team page exists, do not hunt through logged-in LinkedIn, and do not guess names from old articles unless you can confirm they still work there. Write the target title with the name left blank.

## Never do this

- Collect, guess, or write emails or phone numbers, even if they are on the website
- Use Apollo, Prospeo, Lima, Clay, ColdIQ, Hunter, ZoomInfo, Sales Navigator, or similar
- Invent companies or people to pad the list
- Copy a pre-made list from a random "top 100" article without opening each company website
- Deduplicate by company name (Acme Inc and Acme Limited can be different firms; acme.com is the key)
